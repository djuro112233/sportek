# Postnatal care: the first weeks after birth

## What postnatal care is

The postnatal period covers the first six weeks after birth. It is a time of great change and some risk for both mother and newborn. Postnatal care means check-ups by a health worker, support with feeding and recovery, and knowing when to seek help.

## Recommended contacts

- If you gave birth in a facility, you and your baby should stay for at least 24 hours after an uncomplicated birth.
- After going home, WHO recommends at least three more postnatal contacts: between 48 and 72 hours, between 7 and 14 days, and in the sixth week after birth.
- At each contact the health worker checks the mother's bleeding, blood pressure, temperature, wound healing, breasts and mood, and the baby's feeding, weight, breathing, skin colour, umbilical cord and temperature.

## Recovery for the mother

- Vaginal bleeding (lochia) is normal for several weeks and slowly changes from red to pink to yellowish-white.
- Some pain, tiredness and mood swings are common in the first days.
- Rest when you can, eat well, drink enough and accept help.
- Sexual activity can resume when you feel ready; pregnancy is possible again soon after birth, even before periods return, so talk about contraception early.
- If you had a caesarean section, keep the wound clean and dry and avoid heavy lifting.

## Danger signs for the mother: seek care immediately

- Heavy bleeding (soaking more than one pad an hour) or passing large clots.
- Fever, chills, or foul-smelling vaginal discharge.
- Severe headache, blurred vision or convulsions.
- Pain, redness or pus around a wound or in the breast.
- Pain or swelling in one leg, chest pain or difficulty breathing.
- Feeling unable to cope, thoughts of harming yourself or the baby.

## Danger signs for the newborn: seek care immediately

- Not feeding well or stopped feeding.
- Fever or a body that feels cold.
- Fast breathing, chest pulling in, grunting, or pauses in breathing.
- Convulsions or very little movement.
- Yellow skin or eyes in the first day, or deep yellow colour spreading to the palms and soles.
- Redness, swelling or pus around the umbilical cord.
- Many skin pustules, or vomiting everything.

## Caring for the newborn at home

- Keep the baby warm and in skin-to-skin contact; delay the first bath for at least 24 hours.
- Keep the cord stump clean and dry; nothing needs to be applied to it.
- Breastfeed whenever the baby wants, day and night.
- Place the baby on the back to sleep, in a smoke-free room.
- Make sure the baby receives the recommended vaccinations and the newborn screening tests.
