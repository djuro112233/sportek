# Menstrual health

## The menstrual cycle

- Menstruation (a period) is the monthly bleeding from the womb that is part of the reproductive cycle. It usually starts between about 10 and 16 years of age and continues until the menopause.
- A cycle is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. A cycle of 21 to 35 days is typical for adults; in the first years after the first period, cycles are often irregular.
- Bleeding usually lasts between two and seven days.
- Ovulation, the release of an egg, happens roughly in the middle of the cycle; pregnancy is most likely from sex in the days before and around ovulation.

## Managing periods

- Pads, tampons and menstrual cups are all safe options; change them regularly and wash your hands before and after.
- Keep a note of your period dates; a calendar or an app helps you notice changes and gives a clinician useful information.
- Mild cramps, bloating, tender breasts and mood changes before and during a period are common. Warmth, gentle exercise, rest and pain relief recommended by a pharmacist can help.
- Periods are normal and should not stop you going to school, work or sport.

## When to see a health worker

- No period by age 16, or periods that stop for more than three months when you are not pregnant.
- Bleeding so heavy that you soak through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours, or periods that last more than seven days.
- Pain so strong that you cannot do your usual activities even with pain relief.
- Bleeding between periods or after sex.
- A sudden change in your pattern, or signs of anaemia such as tiredness, pale skin and breathlessness.
- A missed period after unprotected sex: do a pregnancy test.

## Myths and facts

- You can get pregnant during a period, especially with short cycles.
- Bathing, washing hair and exercising during a period are safe.
- Hormonal contraception can make periods lighter or stop them; this is not harmful.
- Period pain is real; it is not "in your head".
- Having a period does not make you unclean, and nobody may exclude you from school, prayer or family life because of it.

## Products and access

- If you cannot afford period products, ask at school, a youth centre or a health centre; some provide them free.
- Clean water, soap and a private toilet make managing periods easier; this is a basic right.
