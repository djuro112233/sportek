# Danger signs in pregnancy: when to seek care immediately

## Why this matters

Most pregnancies go well, but some problems need urgent treatment. Knowing the danger signs helps you act fast. If you have any of the signs below, go to the nearest health facility or emergency department straight away, or call emergency services. Do not wait for your next antenatal visit.

## Seek care immediately if you have

- Vaginal bleeding at any stage of pregnancy.
- Severe headache that does not go away, blurred vision, seeing spots or flashing lights.
- Sudden swelling of the face, hands or feet, especially together with headache.
- Severe pain in the belly, or constant pain that does not ease.
- Fever (high temperature) with or without chills.
- Convulsions (fits) or fainting.
- Difficulty breathing or chest pain.
- Fluid leaking from the vagina before labour, especially if it is green or smelly.
- The baby moving much less than usual, or not at all, after you have been feeling movements.
- Regular painful contractions before 37 weeks.
- Vomiting that does not stop, so that you cannot keep fluids down.
- Burning when passing urine together with fever or back pain.

## What these signs can mean

- Bleeding may come from the placenta or, early on, from a miscarriage or an ectopic pregnancy; all need assessment.
- Severe headache, vision changes and swelling can be signs of pre-eclampsia, a condition of high blood pressure in pregnancy that can become dangerous for you and the baby.
- Fever can mean an infection that needs treatment.
- Reduced movements can mean the baby is not getting enough oxygen; the baby's heartbeat needs to be checked.

## What to do

- Go to the nearest health facility or call the emergency number; say that you are pregnant and how many weeks.
- If possible, take your health booklet and a person who can support you.
- Do not take medicines on your own for these signs; the health worker needs to see you first.

## Signs that are usually normal

- Mild nausea, tiredness, mild back pain, occasional light-headedness when standing up and mild ankle swelling in late pregnancy are common.
- Mention them at your next antenatal visit; if they get worse or you are unsure, seek care.
- When in doubt, it is always right to be checked; nobody will blame you for coming in.
