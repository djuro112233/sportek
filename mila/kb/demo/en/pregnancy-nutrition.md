# Nutrition and supplements in pregnancy

## Eating well

- A healthy pregnancy diet includes a variety of foods: vegetables and fruit, whole grains, beans and lentils, dairy, eggs, fish, lean meat and healthy fats.
- Eat regularly; small frequent meals help with nausea.
- Drink plenty of water. Limit sugary drinks and very salty foods.
- You do not need to "eat for two"; the extra energy needed in pregnancy is modest, mainly in the second and third trimesters.
- Healthy weight gain depends on your weight before pregnancy; your health worker will track it with you.

## Supplements recommended in pregnancy

- Iron and folic acid supplements are recommended daily throughout pregnancy to prevent anaemia and birth defects of the brain and spine. Your clinician will tell you the amount and when to start.
- Folic acid is most important in the weeks before and the first weeks after conception, so start as soon as you plan a pregnancy or learn that you are pregnant.
- In places where people get little calcium from food, calcium supplements are recommended to reduce the risk of pre-eclampsia; your clinician will tell you whether this applies to you and the amount.
- Vitamin D, vitamin A and other supplements are not routinely recommended for everyone; take them only when a clinician advises.
- Supplements complement food; they do not replace meals.

## Foods and habits to avoid

- Alcohol: there is no known safe amount in pregnancy.
- Smoking and second-hand smoke harm the baby's growth; ask for help to stop.
- Raw or undercooked meat, raw eggs, unpasteurised milk and cheese, and food that has been left out can carry infections that are dangerous in pregnancy.
- Wash fruit and vegetables and wash your hands before preparing food.
- Limit caffeine (coffee, energy drinks).
- Fish is healthy, but avoid large predatory fish that may contain mercury.

## Common problems

- Nausea and vomiting: eat small dry snacks, avoid strong smells, drink between meals. Seek care if you cannot keep fluids down.
- Heartburn: smaller meals, avoid lying down right after eating.
- Constipation: fibre, water and walking help; iron supplements can make it worse, so tell your clinician.
- Cravings and food aversions are common; eating non-food items such as clay or ice should be mentioned to your clinician as it can signal anaemia.

## Questions to ask at your visit

- Which supplements do I need and in what amount?
- Is my weight gain on track?
- Are there foods I should avoid because of my health condition?
- Can I keep taking the medicines or herbal products I already use?
