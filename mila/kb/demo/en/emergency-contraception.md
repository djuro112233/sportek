# Emergency contraception

## What it is

Emergency contraception is a way to prevent pregnancy after sex that was unprotected or where the method failed, for example a condom that broke or slipped, missed pills, or sex without any method. It is also recommended after sexual assault. It is a back-up method, not a method for regular use.

## Types

- Emergency contraceptive pills: a pill taken as soon as possible after unprotected sex. They mainly work by delaying ovulation. They do not end an existing pregnancy and they do not harm a pregnancy that has already started.
- The copper IUD: a provider can place a copper IUD after unprotected sex; it is the most effective form of emergency contraception and it then provides ongoing contraception for years.

## The time window

- Emergency contraceptive pills can be taken up to five days after unprotected sex, but they work better the sooner they are taken, so it is best not to wait.
- A copper IUD can be placed up to five days after unprotected sex.
- After the time window has passed, emergency contraception is no longer expected to work; a provider can discuss a pregnancy test and other options.

## Where to get it

- Pharmacies: in many places emergency contraceptive pills are available without a prescription; ask the pharmacist.
- Primary health care centres, gynaecologists and family planning services can prescribe pills or place a copper IUD.
- Emergency departments and services for survivors of sexual violence also provide it.
- Anyone of reproductive age, including adolescents, can use it; there are no health conditions that make the pills unsafe.

## After using it

- Your next period may come a little earlier or later than usual. If it is more than a week late, do a pregnancy test.
- Emergency pills do not protect you for the rest of the cycle; use condoms or start a regular method right away.
- If you vomited shortly after taking the pill, ask a pharmacist or clinician whether a repeat is needed.
- Emergency contraception does not protect against sexually transmitted infections; consider testing if there was a risk.

## Good to know

- Using emergency contraception more than once is safe, but a regular method protects you better and is cheaper.
- Side effects, such as nausea, tiredness or spotting, are usually mild and pass within a day or two.
- Nobody can refuse you emergency contraception because of your age or because you are not married.
